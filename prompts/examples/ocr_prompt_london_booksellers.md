You are transcribing pages from the *Post Office London Directory, 1841* (Part 1: Street, Commercial, & Trades Directories) — the Trades' Directory, printed pages 646–649, covering the BOOKSELLERS trade and its immediate neighbours.

The single most valuable and most easily lost information on these pages is the small reference marks printed in front of entry names. Getting those exactly right matters more than anything else in this transcription.

## Page layout

Each page has **three** text columns of roughly equal width, separated by thin vertical rules. Transcribe the entire left column from top to bottom, then the whole middle column, then the whole right column. Text sitting at the same height in two different columns is never one line — never join across columns.

Ignore the running head at the very top of the page: the page number, `TRADES' DIRECTORY`, and the year in brackets (`[1841.` or `1841.]`). Transcribe everything below it.

Output exactly one printed line per output line. When an entry is too long for the column and wraps onto a second, indented line, transcribe that continuation as its own line, exactly as printed — do not join it to the line above.

## Markers — highest priority

Most entries begin with one or more small reference marks indicating the additional trades that firm carried on. They are printed tight against the name, are physically small, and are easy to drop. Transcribe every one of them.

These are the only characters you may emit as markers:

| Emit | Name | How it prints here |
|---|---|---|
| `*` | asterisk | small raised six-pointed star |
| `†` | dagger | upright cross with a short upper arm; prints heavy and can resemble a lowercase `t` or a `+` |
| `‡` | double dagger | dagger with two crossbars; at this size can resemble a capital `I` or a `z` |
| `§` | section sign | doubled looped S; can resemble an `8`, an `&`, or a `$` |
| `‖` | double vertical line | two short parallel uprights |
| `¶` | pilcrow | reversed P with a solid bowl; can resemble a heavy `T`, a `1`, or a filled square |
| `a` `b` `c` `d` `e` | superscript letters | small raised roman letters; emit as plain lowercase letters |

Rules for markers:

- **Never omit one.** If a line begins with marks, the transcribed line must begin with those marks. Do not start the line at the name.
- **Preserve the exact order** as printed. The order varies between entries and is not alphabetical or canonical — `*‡`, `‖¶`, `§‖¶`, `†§`, `‖‡b` all occur. Do not reorder, deduplicate, sort, or normalise.
- **No spaces** between markers, or between the last marker and the name. Transcribe `‖†‡¶Ackermann & Co. 96 Strand`, not `‖ † ‡ ¶ Ackermann & Co. 96 Strand`.
- Emit `‖` as the **single character** `‖` — never as two `|` characters and never as `II` or `ll`.
- **Never invent a marker.** A large share of entries carry none at all; those lines begin directly with the name.
- Superscript letters are markers like any other and may be mixed in with the symbols: `dBryant John, 9 King William st. Strand`, `§‖aPaine Geo. 46 High st. Marylebone`, `‖‡bDykes & Cooper, 33 Piccadilly`.
- The only letters that can appear as markers are `a` `b` `c` `d` `e`. A dagger `†` printed at this size closely resembles a lowercase `t` — never transcribe a marker as `t`, `f`, `i`, or any other letter. If a mark looks like a `t`, it is a `†`. `e†Carpenter N. 60 Spencer st.` must not become `etCarpenter N.`
- `†` and `‖` are the two most frequently confused marks here, and `‖` is the one most often missed altogether. Before moving on from a line, check the left edge specifically for a `‖` — two parallel uprights of equal height, distinct from the single-stroke `†` with its crossbar.

**The column rule is not a marker.** In the middle and right columns a thin vertical rule runs down the page immediately to the left of the first character of every line. It is page furniture. Do not transcribe it, and never render it as `‖` or `|`. Emit `‖` only where two short parallel strokes sit clearly inside the column, to the right of the rule, directly against the start of a name — and where the same mark is absent from neighbouring lines. If every line in a column appears to start with the same upright stroke, that stroke is the rule, not a marker.

## Keys and legends

Some sub-headings are followed by legend lines of the form `Marked thus † are Publishers.` These lines define what the marks mean and are critical. Transcribe every one verbatim, one per output line, keeping the symbol exactly as printed. In these lines the symbol is set with a space on either side — keep that spacing.

Also transcribe `See also …` and `See … Directory` cross-reference lines verbatim as their own lines.

## Headings

Three heading levels appear, all of which must be transcribed on their own lines:

- Primary trade, large bold capitals — `BOOKSELLERS.`, `BOOKBINDERS' PRESS MAKERS.`
- Continuation of a trade across a page or column break — `BOOKSELLERS, &c.—continued.`
- Sub-category, bold mixed case with an em dash — `Booksellers—Medical.`, `Booksellers—Foreign.`, `Booksellers in General.`

Use an em dash (`—`) in headings where the page prints one. Where a heading wraps onto two printed lines, transcribe it as two lines.

## Entry text

- Preserve abbreviations exactly: `st.`, `sq.`, `pl.`, `rd.`, `ter.`, `gdn`, `blds.`, `Gt.`, `Up.`, `nth`, `who.`, `&c.` Do not expand or correct them.
- Preserve internal `&`, and both `M'` and `Mc` name forms as printed.
- Preserve parenthetical qualifiers, which are printed in italics: `(oriental)`, `(who.)`, `(old)`, `(catholic)`, `(military)`, `(bible)`, `(& foreign)`, `(american com. agents)`, `(publishers of Pigot & Co.'s directories)`. Keep the parentheses; do not italicise, expand, or move them.
- Preserve multiple addresses in a single entry as printed: `Coghlan F. 5 Brydges st. & 457 Strand`.
- Preserve inline cross-references as printed: `Norie John W. & Co. see Wilson Charles`.
- Preserve the page's own spacing quirks in numbers and abbreviations (`24 Haymrkt.&1 Panton st`) rather than tidying them.

## Degraded text

These pages are dense and the type is worn. Give your best reading of blurred characters rather than skipping them. Use `[illegible]` only for a span you genuinely cannot read at all after a real attempt, and `[blank]` only for a region that genuinely contains no text. Never use either token in place of a marker you can partially see — transcribe your best reading of the mark instead.

Do not transcribe handwritten annotations, stamps, or margin notes.

## Output

Plain text only. One printed line per output line. No markdown, no bold or italics, no page or column labels, no commentary.
